# Candidate 2: edge-adaptive original-pixel sampling

Status: implementation complete; focused synthetic verification passed (11 tests). No full-scene execution, production changes, or visual-quality claim by this worker.

`adaptive.select(data, budget)` returns C-order original-grid indices, one positive U/V multiplier pair per selected point, and JSON-safe statistics. It reads `depth`, `valid`, and `spacing`; source mapping remains recoverable from the indices. The official budget is exactly 217,049. Original depths, camera parameters, validity, and source images are unchanged.

The algorithm reserves one observation per nonempty 16×16 image tile whenever the budget permits. Capped proportional largest-remainder allocation distributes the remaining budget across tiles. Pixel weights are 4 on depth/validity/image boundaries, 2 within a two-pixel Manhattan halo, and 1 elsewhere. Within each tile, Morton-ordered systematic sampling uses inclusion probabilities `min(1, rate * weight)` summing to its integer quota. This is deterministic sampling without replacement and remains exact when some pixels saturate at probability 1. Bounded weights and tile reservations prevent a few high-scoring boundaries from consuming the full budget.

A depth link breaks when its jump exceeds `max(2 * spacing, 0.005 * min_depth) + 3 * smaller_neighboring_depth_slope`. The slope term avoids treating a consistent sloped plane as a discontinuity. This is a heuristic, not an estimated geometric boundary label.

Nominal U/V multipliers are the inverse square root of local inclusion probability. Each axis is independently limited by its distance to a broken pixel interface or image edge, with a 0.95 margin and the existing 1.05 disc radius. Final scales stay in [0.5, 8]. The 0.5 lower bound preserves meaningful area even where a centered symmetric disc cannot fit inside the observed support. Those points are counted explicitly, and the existing original-source four-sample texture gate still decides whether their triangles can be textured. Axiswise limits do not prove support at diagonal corners or on curved surfaces.

The coverage hypothesis is that additional, smaller boundary surfels retain detail and textured area where stride-4 discs cross visibility discontinuities, while somewhat larger interior footprints preserve broad planar coverage. The tradeoff is lower interior sample density, possible directional holes, and more observations spent on validity noise or image borders. Tile occupancy is an image-space coverage proxy; only coordinator raycasts and visual comparisons can establish useful scene coverage and boundary behavior.

Memory and work are linear in the dense grid plus selected points; sorting is confined to a fixed 256-pixel Morton template, tile apportionment, and the final selected index list. No global nearest-neighbor search, per-pixel priority heap, model calls, or additional dependencies are introduced. Full-scene selection/export time remains unmeasured here.
