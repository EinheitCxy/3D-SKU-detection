Each `--run-dir` must be a new immediate task-local directory. No previous run is overwritten or automatically retried. The runner writes `partial.json` before each arm, after export (before comparison), after comparison, on catchable signals and on failures. A failed arm retains its raw elapsed export duration, captured-page count, resource receipt and error; signal interruption also saves elapsed duration at signal delivery before encoder shutdown. SIGKILL, the CPU hard-limit kill or a process crash cannot guarantee a final receipt; the most recently written partial remains available. No success is inferred from a partial sequence.

Limits cover the entire runner process, including imports, exports, copies and comparisons: smoke 100 CPU seconds / 120 wall seconds; official 2,000 CPU seconds / 900 wall seconds; 12 GiB address space and RSS; 256 MiB per file. RLIMIT_CPU sends a catchable signal one CPU second before the budget and hard-kills at the budget. ITIMER_REAL requests shutdown one second before the wall ceiling; the required external `timeout` wrapper terminates the complete uv command group and hard-kills after that one-second grace period. Preserve the wrapper when executing these commands, since an interrupted exporter drains running encoders. RSS is sampled every 0.1 seconds, supplemented by Linux `RUSAGE_SELF.ru_maxrss`; sampled peaks can miss shorter intervals. CPU receipts report self user+system and reaped-child user+system separately and together. The runner launches no subprocesses and treats an observed child as failure. Numerical/encoder/sampler threads share the fixed CPU set; no GPU calls occur.

Only one export runs at a time. A single temporary BIN and a single current GLB are the only simultaneously large files (at most 2 × 256 MiB); B1 GLB bytes plus baseline/current page captures are held in RAM. The production encoder queue bounds raw pages at two/four including running tasks, approximately 40/80 MiB at roughly 20 MiB per full raw page. These are raw-page bounds, not total RSS: source arrays, baker/Pillow workspaces, encoded bytes and full comparison captures add memory. Only the successfully compared current GLB is deleted, and current capture references are released before the next arm. Failed outputs remain reviewable and halt the sequence. Each receipt is capped at 1 MiB (plus its atomic replacement file); inputs, orientation sidecars and old reports are never written.
